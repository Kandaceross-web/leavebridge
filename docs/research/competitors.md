# LeaveBridge Health: Competitor Research

> Note (2026-10-09): written before the owner set the price to $279.99 with recertification at $119.99 and follow-up forms at $9.99, and before the owner (an LPC licensed in MS and TN) signed off on LPC certification under 29 CFR 825.125(b)(4). References to $275 or to MS needing a clinician predate those decisions. The compact research (compact-states.md) later found Tennessee went live in the Counseling Compact in September 2026; Mississippi is not live yet.

Research date: 2026-10-08. Method: WebSearch (standard, plus extended where results were thin) and WebFetch. Fetched pages were turned into text by a summarizing fetch tool. Quotes are as the tool returned them. Re-check them before using them in public copy.

**Labels:** OBSERVED = seen on the page or source (URL given). SUPPORTED = several sources agree. INFERRED = my reasoning from observed facts. HYPOTHESIS = needs testing.

**Caveats**
- WebFetch strips `<script>` tags, so JSON-LD and schema.org markup could not be checked on any site. Only ratings shown on the page are recorded.
- Search results for this topic contain a lot of SEO spam, such as demo.appwrite.io pages. All of it was ignored.
- Reddit could not be searched (the search proxy rejected the request). Teamblind threads were used as the public-forum source instead.
- Pages that could not be fetched:
  - fmla.doctor (JavaScript-only app; no content)
  - fmlamd.com (blocked by robots.txt; only its Trustpilot page was used)
  - fmlanow.com/eligible-conditions/anxiety/ (fetch error)
  - doctorondemand.com/doctors-note/ (metadata only)
  - one teamblind thread (fetch error)
- Cerebral, Brightside, Done and Teladoc policy pages on FMLA forms could not be found. Search returned only spam.

  - The article does not say whether a separately billed evaluation is allowed, or whether telehealth is covered.
- HYPOTHESIS: A $275 bundled price may conflict with the cap for Nevada patients, unless the assessment and the certification fee are priced separately. This needs legal review.

## 5. Gaps: what no competitor explains well, and where LeaveBridge could be better

1. **Clinical independence** (INFERRED). Only Amica ("completed only when clinically supported") and MyFMLA say plainly that the clinician may decline. FMLA mills present certification as the product ("Get Certified for $49", "Get Approved… Today"). No one explains *how* the clinician decides, or what happens to your money and your records if they decline.
2. **Transparent, total price** (SUPPORTED).
   - FMLADocs hides its price.
   - FMLA NOW has an undisclosed "membership".
   - Trust Medical and MyFMLA use deposits plus balances, and charge for follow-ups.
   - Reviewers complain about surprise recurring charges.
   - Only Amica ($450 / $600) and MyFMLA list clear prices.
   - LeaveBridge's single $275 price that includes the forms would be clearly better, but only if it states what happens for extensions, employer clarifications, return-to-work forms and additional forms.
3. **What is not guaranteed** (INFERRED). The category splits into two groups:
   - Guarantee-led: FMLADocs "Approved or your money back"; Trust Medical "98% Employer Approval Rate"; FMLA NOW "100% Risk Free".
   - Honest disclaimers: Amica, MyFMLA, Advanced Client Care, Psychiatry Relief.
   - No one explains in plain language who actually decides: employer or leave administrator for FMLA, insurer for STD, state for SDI/PFL.
4. **Refund policy when the clinician says no** (INFERRED).
   - Amica, MyFMLA and Psychiatry Relief show no refund policy.
   - Advanced Client Care is non-refundable after session 1.
   - Guarantee-led sites tie refunds to *outcome*, which can create pressure to certify (INFERRED).
   - LeaveBridge could publish a clear, outcome-neutral refund rule. For example: you pay for the assessment, and something defined happens if documentation is not supported. Whether that wins users needs testing (HYPOTHESIS).
5. **Privacy: what the employer sees** (INFERRED). Only Advanced Client Care says the full assessment stays internal. Only RealDoctorsNotes mentions not showing "sensitive diagnoses". No one explains the minimum-necessary disclosure on WH-380-E, or that employers are not entitled to a diagnosis. This is a strong differentiator for mental-health users who fear stigma.
6. **Licensed where you are** (SUPPORTED).
   - Most direct competitors say "licensed across the U.S." or "Licensed in your state" without showing licences.
   - FMLADocs' named reviewer is licensed in Delaware only.
   - Amica shows a licence number and PSYPACT ID.
   - A per-state page that names each clinician's licence type and number, and that requires you to be physically in the state, would beat everyone.
7. **Mental-health specialism** (INFERRED). The FMLA mills are general-medicine physicians. Talkspace and BetterHelp refuse. Forum users say therapists and LMFTs often can't or won't sign. LeaveBridge's licensed mental-health clinicians sit between these. Note that LMFT or counsellor signatures may be rejected by some leave administrators (OBSERVED in the Teamblind quote). LeaveBridge should state which credential signs each form type.
8. **Human support and follow-through** (SUPPORTED by reviews). The top complaints are unreachable staff, paperwork that never arrives, and employer clarification requests left unanswered. A promised response time and a named workflow for employer or insurer clarifications would answer the category's biggest pain.
9. **Safety scope** (OBSERVED gap). Only Amica gives crisis routing (988/911) and states what it does not do. LeaveBridge's "not therapy, urgent care, or medication management" fits this pattern, and few competitors say it.
10. **Price position** (INFERRED). $275 sits above the mills ($49 to $250) and well below a psychologist evaluation ($450 to $600). It needs to be justified by mental-health specialisation and its stated policies. Whether users will pay that premium is a HYPOTHESIS to test.
